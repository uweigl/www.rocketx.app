/ WHY NATIVE

Your catalog belongs on the home screen — not in a browser tab.

A responsive website and a native app are not the same product. One waits to be found. The other sits in your buyer's pocket and asks for the reorder.

01

Reordering is a habit, not a search.

Most B2B orders are repeat orders. A browser makes your buyer find a bookmark, load a site, and log in every single time. An app is one tap from the home screen, already signed in. You remove that friction from every reorder for the life of the account.

02

You get a permanent place on the buyer's phone.

Your icon sits on the home screen next to their bank and their email. Competitors have to be searched for and rediscovered. That default position is worth more than any single campaign — and it compounds every week it stays there.

03

Push brings buyers back without a sales call.

Back-in-stock alerts, contract renewals, price changes, and reorder reminders reach the buyer directly. Airship finds that users who opt into push retain at two to three times the rate of those who don't. Email gets filtered; a push lands.

04

The phone is where the work actually happens.

Buyers order from warehouse aisles, loading docks, job sites, and trade-show floors — not from a desk. Native access to the camera means barcode scanning and instant reorder from a shelf label. A browser tab cannot reach that hardware reliably.

05

It keeps working when the signal doesn't.

Steel-walled warehouses, basements, and rural delivery routes kill mobile reception. A native app holds the catalog, pricing, and cart locally and syncs when the connection returns. A web cart in that moment is simply a lost order.

06

Millions of SKUs stay instant.

Native apps cache and index locally instead of making a network round trip on every keystroke. At catalog sizes where web storefronts start to stall, search stays sub-second — which is exactly when a buyer decides whether to keep going or call somebody.
